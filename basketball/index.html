For grown-ups

Super Basketball teaches the basic rules of youth basketball in short lessons. Coach Buddy, the dog in the orange cap, reads every line out loud, so kids who aren't reading yet can play on their own. Each finished lesson earns a gold star.

The lessons

  1. The Court. Two baskets: your team shoots at the other team's basket and defends its own. The boundary lines, the half-court line, the center circle (where the jump ball starts the game), the key and the free-throw line. Teams switch baskets at halftime. Guards, forwards and the center.
  2. Dribble! Move with the ball by bouncing it with one hand. Walking or running while holding the ball is traveling. Once you stop dribbling, pass or shoot. Finish with a layup.
  3. Shoot! Bend your knees, look at the hoop, push the ball up and follow through. A basket is 2 points. A player fouled while shooting gets free throws: 1 point each, and nobody can guard the shooter.
  4. Travel or Double? Traveling (walking or running while holding the ball; standing still and pivoting on one foot is fine) and double dribble (dribbling with two hands, or dribbling again after stopping). After the call, the other team gets the ball. Then your child is the referee for six plays.
  5. Defense & Fouls. Stand between your player and the basket, knees bent, hands up. Pushing, holding, grabbing and slapping arms are fouls; hands straight up, standing your ground and a clean steal are good defense.
  6. Pass & Rebound. Pass to the open teammate, grab rebounds after a missed shot, and out of bounds: the line itself is out, and the team that did not touch the ball last throws it in.

Play Hoops is a 3-on-3 game against the Dinos with two short halves (1, 2 or 3 minutes each, on a running clock). It starts with a jump ball, a basket counts 2 points and a free throw 1, and the teams switch baskets at halftime. The Dinos sometimes travel, double dribble or step out of bounds, and the referee explains each call.

Controls

Tap or click. On offense, your child controls whoever has the ball: tap the floor to dribble there, a teammate to pass, or the hoop to shoot (tap when the arrow on the SHOOT! meter is in the green). On defense, tap the Dino with the ball to try a steal. With a keyboard: Space dribbles toward the basket and shoots when close, tries a steal on defense, and continues; 1 2 pass to a teammate, arrow keys move between buttons, and Esc goes back to the menu.

At the real court

  • Practice dribbling in place first, then while walking, then while jogging. Fingertips, not palms, and eyes up.
  • A handy phrase for games: "stop, then pass or shoot." It prevents most double dribbles.
  • Leagues for 5 to 7 year olds differ a lot. Many use 8-foot hoops and a smaller ball, keep the clock running, skip free throws or let kids re-do a violation, and only play man-to-man defense (no full-court press). Referees are usually lenient at this age. The game doesn't teach the 3-, 5- and 10-second rules or the backcourt rule, which most beginner leagues don't enforce.
  • Shots from behind the big arc count 3 points for older players; many young leagues don't count threes.
