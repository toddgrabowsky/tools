For grown-ups

Super Soccer teaches the basic rules of youth soccer in short lessons. Coach Buddy, the dog in the cap, reads every line out loud, so kids who aren't reading yet can play on their own. Each finished lesson earns a gold star.

The lessons

  1. The Field. The two goals, which way your team attacks (teams switch ends at halftime), the boundary lines, the center circle and kickoff, the goal box, and the four positions: goalie, defender, midfielder and forward.
  2. Dribble! Move the ball with your feet, not your hands (only the goalie may handle it). Dribbling means lots of little kicks that keep the ball close. Dribble through a cone course, then shoot.
  3. Shoot & Score. Aim for a corner, away from the goalie. The whole ball has to cross the goal line, between the posts and under the crossbar. After a goal, the other team kicks off.
  4. In or Out? The ball is out only when the whole ball is over the line, and a ball on the line is still in. Throw-ins (two hands, from behind the head, both feet on the ground), goal kicks and corner kicks, and why it matters who touched the ball last.
  5. Fair Play. No pushing, tripping, holding or handballs, and the other team gets a free kick. A fair tackle plays the ball, not the player. The goalie may use hands only inside the goal box. Help up a player who falls.
  6. Pass & Defend. Spread out instead of chasing the ball in a swarm, pass to an open teammate, and defend "goal side" (between the ball and your goal).

Play Soccer is a 4 v 4 match against the Dinos, with a goalie and three field players on each side. Pick a short, medium or long game: two halves of 1, 2 or 3 minutes on a running clock that pauses while the coach talks. There are kickoffs, throw-ins, goal kicks, corner kicks and the odd Dino foul (a free kick for your team), and the teams switch ends at halftime. The game is tuned so your child usually wins, but the Dinos can score.

Controls

Tap or click. Your child controls the teammate closest to the ball, marked YOU. With the ball, tap the field to dribble there, tap a teammate to pass, or tap the goal to shoot. Without it, the player runs toward the ball on their own, and tapping the Dino with the ball sends them in for a tackle. With a keyboard: Space continues and does the main action (kick, shoot or pass), 1 2 3 pick answers and teammates, arrow keys move between buttons, and Esc goes back to the menu.

At the real field

  • Rules vary by league. Many U6 and U7 games are 3 v 3 or 4 v 4 with small goals and no goalies, and goalies usually start around U8. The lessons explain what a goalie does, and the match uses them.
  • Offside usually isn't called for young kids (often not until U10), so the game leaves it out. Heading isn't allowed for young players either (US Soccer bans it for ages 10 and under), so the game never shows it.
  • Many leagues don't keep score for the youngest players, and details like penalty kicks, build-out lines and throw-in do-overs differ from league to league.
  • Try it in the yard: dribble through a line of cones (or shoes) with little touches, or play "red light, green light" dribbling, where "red light!" means stop the ball with the bottom of your foot.
