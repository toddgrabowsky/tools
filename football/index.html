For grown-ups

Super Football teaches the basic rules of youth flag football (5 on 5, no tackling), based on the NFL FLAG rules many youth leagues use. Coach Buddy, the dog in the cap, reads every line out loud, so kids who aren't reading yet can play on their own. Each finished lesson earns a gold star.

The lessons

  1. The Field. The two end zones, which way your team runs to score, yard lines and midfield, and the five positions: center, quarterback, running back and receivers.
  2. Hike & Run. Huddle, line up at the line of scrimmage, hike the ball, take a handoff, pick the right end zone, and run around the defenders for a touchdown.
  3. Pull the Flag. No tackling or pushing: you stop the runner by pulling a flag, and the ball is placed where the flag came off. Keeping your hands and the ball away from your own flags (no "flag guarding").
  4. Four Downs. Four tries to get past midfield, then three more to score. What down is it now? What happens if you don't make it (the other team gets the ball).
  5. Pass & Catch. One forward pass from behind the line, thrown to a teammate who is open. Interceptions, and incomplete passes (the ball goes back and it's the next down).
  6. Touchdown! Wait for the hike (no offside), a touchdown is 6 points, and the extra-point try: 1 point from the 5-yard line or 2 points from the 10.

Game Time is a short game against the Dinos. Pick short, medium or long (2, 3 or 4 drives for each team). Every drive starts at the team's own 5-yard line with four downs to cross midfield and three more to score, as in NFL FLAG, and there are no kickoffs. After a touchdown, your child picks a 1-point or 2-point try. On offense, pick RUN or PASS, tap HIKE, then tap the field to steer the runner, or tap an open teammate to throw. A rusher starts 7 yards back, so throw before the countdown runs out. On defense, your child's player chases the ball carrier; tapping the runner makes them sprint, and tapping when the ring turns yellow pulls the flag.

Controls

Tap or click. With a keyboard: Space continues, hikes the ball and pulls a flag when the ring is yellow, 1 2 3 pick a receiver to throw to, arrow keys move between buttons, and Esc goes back to the menu.

At the real field

  • Practice flag pulling in the yard: tuck a dish towel into a waistband and take turns chasing and grabbing it. Remind them: grab the flag, never the shirt or the player.
  • Before each game or half, ask "Which way are we going?" In the game your team always runs to the right; real teams switch ends at halftime, so kids need to check.
  • Leagues differ. Field sizes vary (often about 25-30 yards wide and 50-70 yards long), some play 7 on 7, many have "no-run zones" near midfield and the end zone where the offense must pass, some don't let the quarterback run, and pass clocks range from about 4 to 7 seconds.
  • The game keeps a few things simple. When a team runs out of downs or throws an interception, the other team always starts at its own 5. In NFL FLAG, a team can choose to "punt" on fourth down, which works that way; if it goes for it and misses, the other team takes over where the play ended, and interceptions can be run back. There are also no no-run zones or safeties, and penalties only come up in the lessons.
